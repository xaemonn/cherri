Most period changes are harmless, but some need a doctor soon, and a few need emergency care.

## Get emergency help now (call 108 or 112)
- Heavy bleeding with dizziness, fainting or a racing heart
- Severe pain on one side of the lower belly, especially with a missed period, shoulder-tip pain or bleeding (possible ectopic pregnancy)
- Heavy bleeding or severe pain during pregnancy
- Thoughts of harming yourself (or call Tele-MANAS on 14416)

## See a doctor soon
- No period for 3 months or more (and not pregnant)
- Periods less than 21 days apart, or more than 35 days apart for several months
- Bleeding for more than 7 days, or soaking a pad every 1 to 2 hours
- Bleeding between periods, after sex, or any bleeding after menopause
- Period pain that stops you from daily activities or isn't helped by painkillers
- Pain during sex
- A new lump in the breast or armpit
- Unusual vaginal discharge with itching, a bad smell or pelvic pain

## Making the visit count
Bring your tracking report, write down your top 3 concerns, and ask what the possible causes are and what tests you need. If you feel dismissed, it is okay to say "This is affecting my life and I'd like it investigated", or to get a second opinion.
