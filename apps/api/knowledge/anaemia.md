Anaemia means your blood has too little haemoglobin to carry enough oxygen. It is extremely common in India: the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019–21) found about 57% of women aged 15 to 49 were anaemic. The most common cause is iron deficiency.

## Symptoms
Tiredness and weakness, breathlessness or a racing heart on light effort, dizziness, pale skin or inner eyelids, cold hands and feet, hair fall, brittle or spoon-shaped nails, headaches and cravings for ice, mud, chalk or raw rice (called pica). Mild anaemia can have no symptoms at all.

## Common causes
Heavy or frequent periods, low iron in the diet, pregnancy, poor absorption (for example drinking tea with meals), worm infections, and low vitamin B12 or folate.

## Tests
A complete blood count (CBC) shows haemoglobin. For non-pregnant women, haemoglobin below 12 g/dL is anaemia. Serum ferritin shows iron stores and can be low even when haemoglobin is still normal.

## Treatment
Iron tablets prescribed by a doctor, usually for at least 3 months to refill stores, plus treating the cause, such as heavy periods. Under the Anemia Mukt Bharat programme, iron-folic acid supplements and testing are free at government health facilities. Severe anaemia (haemoglobin below 8 g/dL) needs prompt medical care.
