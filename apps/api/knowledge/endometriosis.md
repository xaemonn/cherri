Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside it, causing inflammation and pain. The WHO estimates it affects roughly 10% of women and girls of reproductive age worldwide. Diagnosis is often delayed by years because severe period pain is wrongly treated as normal.

## Symptoms
Period pain that disrupts daily life, pain not relieved by painkillers, pelvic pain outside periods, deep pain during or after sex, painful bowel movements or peeing around periods, tiredness, and difficulty getting pregnant. Symptoms vary a lot from person to person.

## Getting diagnosed
NICE guidance says doctors should suspect endometriosis when these symptoms are present. A doctor may do a pelvic exam and an ultrasound, but a normal scan does not rule it out. MRI or laparoscopy (keyhole surgery) may be needed. Keeping a pain diary helps your doctor take your symptoms seriously.

## Treatment
Pain relief, hormonal treatments that reduce or stop periods, and surgery to remove endometriosis tissue. Treatment depends on your symptoms and whether you want to get pregnant. Support from a gynaecologist experienced in endometriosis makes a difference.
