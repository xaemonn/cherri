A menstrual cycle runs from the first day of one period to the day before the next. For adults a cycle between 21 and 35 days is typical, and periods usually last 2 to 7 days. In the first few years after periods start, cycles of up to 45 days can be normal.

## The four phases
- **Menstrual phase (period):** the lining of the uterus sheds. Hormones are at their lowest.
- **Follicular phase:** oestrogen rises and an egg matures. Many people feel energy and mood lift.
- **Ovulation:** an egg is released, usually about 14 days before the next period, not on day 14 of every cycle. This is the most fertile time.
- **Luteal phase:** progesterone rises. If there is no pregnancy, hormones fall and the next period starts. PMS symptoms happen in this phase.

## What counts as normal
Cycle length can vary by a few days from month to month. A difference of more than about 7 to 9 days between your shortest and longest cycles is called irregular. Losing about 2 to 3 tablespoons of blood over a period is typical.

## Why tracking helps
Logging your periods and symptoms shows your own normal, so changes stand out. Doctors find a record of cycle dates, flow and pain far more useful than memory.
