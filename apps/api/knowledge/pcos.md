PCOS is a common hormonal condition that affects how the ovaries work. Many people with PCOS don't know they have it. In India it is often called PCOD.

## Symptoms
Irregular or missed periods, excess facial or body hair, acne, scalp hair thinning, weight gain, dark velvety skin patches (a sign of insulin resistance), and difficulty getting pregnant. Not everyone has every symptom.

## How it is diagnosed
Doctors usually use the Rotterdam criteria. PCOS is diagnosed when at least 2 of these 3 are present, after ruling out other causes such as thyroid problems or high prolactin:
1. Irregular or absent ovulation (irregular periods)
2. Signs of high androgens (excess hair, acne, scalp thinning) or high testosterone on a blood test
3. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound

## Managing PCOS
There is no cure, but symptoms can be managed well. Regular physical activity, balanced meals and good sleep improve hormones and insulin resistance. For people who are overweight, even a 5 to 10% weight loss can bring back regular cycles. Doctors may suggest hormonal pills to regulate periods, metformin for insulin resistance, treatments for acne and hair, and medicines to help ovulation when trying to conceive.

## Long-term health
PCOS raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol and, if periods are very infrequent, thickening of the uterine lining. Regular blood sugar checks are a good idea.
