There are many safe contraception options. The best one depends on your health, how often you have sex, and whether you want children later. Several methods are free at government health facilities.

## Common methods
- **Condoms:** the only method that also protects against sexually transmitted infections.
- **Combined oral contraceptive pills:** taken daily. They can also make periods lighter and less painful.
- **Weekly pill (Chhaya / centchroman):** a non-hormonal pill offered free in the government programme.
- **Copper IUD (Copper-T):** a small device placed in the uterus that works for 5 to 10 years. Periods may become heavier.
- **Hormonal IUD:** works for several years and often makes periods much lighter.
- **Injectable (Antara):** an injection every 3 months, available in the government programme.

## Emergency contraception
Emergency pills work best as soon as possible after unprotected sex, ideally within 72 hours. They are not a regular method and don't cause abortion.

## Myths
Contraceptive pills don't cause infertility: fertility returns after stopping. A doctor or health centre can help you choose safely, especially if you have migraines with aura, high blood pressure or a clotting history.
