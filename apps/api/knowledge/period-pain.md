Period pain is common. It is usually felt as cramps in the lower belly that can spread to the back and thighs. It normally starts when bleeding begins and lasts 2 to 3 days.

## What helps
- **Anti-inflammatory painkillers** such as ibuprofen or mefenamic acid work best when taken as soon as the pain starts. Follow the label or your doctor's advice, and avoid them if you have stomach ulcers or asthma made worse by these medicines.
- **Heat:** a hot water bag or warm bath on the lower belly relaxes the muscles.
- **Gentle exercise:** walking, stretching and yoga can reduce cramps.
- **Hormonal contraception** can make periods lighter and less painful. Discuss it with a doctor.

## When pain is not normal
See a doctor if pain stops you from going to school, college or work, if painkillers don't help, if pain happens outside your period, or if you have pain during sex or when pooping. These can be signs of endometriosis, adenomyosis or fibroids, which are treatable.
