Cervical cancer is one of the most common cancers in Indian women, yet it is one of the most preventable. Almost all cases are caused by long-lasting infection with human papillomavirus (HPV).

## HPV vaccine
The vaccine works best at ages 9 to 14, before any sexual contact, but older girls and young women can still benefit. India has its own HPV vaccine (Cervavac), and the government has been rolling it out for adolescent girls.

## Screening
Screening finds changes before they become cancer. India's national programme recommends screening women aged 30 to 65, using visual inspection (VIA), a Pap smear or an HPV test. Marital status doesn't matter: anyone who has ever been sexually active should be screened.

## Warning signs
Bleeding after sex, between periods or after menopause, unusual discharge and pelvic pain should be checked by a doctor.
